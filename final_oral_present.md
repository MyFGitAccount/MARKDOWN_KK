## SLIDE 4: Problem 1 - Course Scheduling is Manual and Difficult

**Visual:** List of current student actions, Why This Is A Problem box

**Speaker Says (Xavier):**

"Let me walk you through what students currently do to plan their schedules. First, you download multiple PDF timetable files from the college portal. Then you open each PDF separately, sometimes for different days or different departments. You read through rows and rows of course codes, times, and room numbers. You try to compare courses across different documents to find available slots. You check for time conflicts by scanning everything manually with your eyes. You also have to track prerequisites and course dependencies separately. And if your course selections change? You repeat the entire process again.

Why is this such a big problem? First, there is no visual overview. You cannot easily see what your week will look like. Second, there is time pressure. The add-drop period is only a few days long, which adds a lot of stress. And third, human error is very common. When you check everything manually, you are very likely to make mistakes and end up with overlapping schedules."

## SLIDE 5: Existing Tools Each Have Limitations

**Visual:** Table comparing Moodle, Google Calendar, PDF Files, Spreadsheets

**Speaker Says (Xavier):**

"Now, you might be thinking, don't we already have tools for this? Let me explain why existing tools are not enough.

Google Calendar has a great visual interface, but it has no connection to HKU SPACE course data. You have to manually type in every single course yourself.

PDF files show the timetable as a static document. There is no interaction, no automation, and it is very difficult to compare across different files.

Spreadsheets let you manually track your schedule, but you have to enter all the data yourself. It is error-prone and has no automatic conflict detection.

So here is the critical gap: We need a tool that combines institutional course data with a visual, interactive scheduling interface. That is exactly what our Timetable Planner does."

## SLIDE 6: Problem 2 - Finding Study Partners Is Unstructured

**Visual:** List of current student actions, Why This Is A Problem box

**Speaker Says (Xavier):**

"Now let me talk about the second problem - finding study partners.

**Visual:** List of current student actions, Why This Is A Problem box

## SLIDE 15: The Credit Economy - Give to Get

**Speaker Says (Xavier):**

"Let me explain how the credit economy works. It is very simple.

## SLIDE 36: FR6 - The Give-to-Get Credit System

**Visual:** Credit Mechanics table and explanation

**Speaker Says (Kwok):**

"Functional Requirement six is the Credit System. This is the heart of the questionnaire exchange.

Here are the credit mechanics. New user registration gives you three credits. Posting a questionnaire costs one credit. Filling someone else's survey earns you one credit. All credit changes use atomic transactions, which ensures database integrity.
